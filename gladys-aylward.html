THE HIDDEN STORY

Gladys Aylward

Born: February 24, 1902

Died: January 3, 1970

Field: Missions

Known For: Rescue of Children in China

In the 1920s, a young English woman became convinced that her life belonged somewhere far beyond the streets of her home country. Her name was Gladys Aylward. She had not grown up expecting to become a missionary, and she had no university education or wealthy family to support her ambitions. Yet she eventually traveled thousands of kilometers to China, where she spent decades serving people, caring for children, and helping others through some of the country's most difficult years.

Gladys was born in London in 1902. She grew up in a working-class family and worked as a domestic servant when she was a young woman. During this period, she became increasingly interested in Christianity and began feeling that she should become a missionary. She wanted to travel to China, a country that had captured her imagination through stories she had read and heard.

Missionary organizations, however, did not immediately accept her. Gladys lacked the formal education and training normally expected of missionaries. Instead of abandoning the idea, she saved her money and looked for another way to reach China.

In 1930, Gladys used her savings to purchase a one-way railway ticket that would take her across Europe and Asia toward China. The journey was long and difficult, and she eventually reached the country by traveling through Russia. She arrived with very little money and no guarantee that the life she had imagined would actually work out.

Gladys eventually joined another British missionary, Jeannie Lawson, in the remote town of Yangcheng in Shanxi Province. Lawson was already running a small mission and inn, and Gladys helped her serve travelers and local people. After Lawson died, Gladys continued the work herself.

She learned to speak Chinese and adapted to local customs. Rather than remaining separated from the community around her, she spent time talking with ordinary people and building relationships. Her ability to communicate with local people became one of her greatest strengths.

Gladys became particularly known for her work with children. She opened her home to orphans and children who had been abandoned or separated from their families. Her small mission gradually became a place where vulnerable children could find food, shelter, education, and care.

Her work became even more difficult after the Second Sino-Japanese War expanded and Japanese forces invaded large parts of China. Cities and villages were affected by fighting, and ordinary families faced displacement, poverty, and violence.

Gladys continued helping people despite the danger. She worked with local communities and became involved in efforts to care for children caught in the conflict. Her mission was no longer simply a place for religious work. It had become a refuge for people who desperately needed protection.

One of the most difficult moments of her life came in 1938, when Japanese aircraft bombed Yangcheng. Gladys survived the attack, but the violence made it clear that remaining in the area was becoming increasingly dangerous.

Gladys eventually found herself responsible for a large group of children. She faced the terrifying problem of getting them away from the fighting. With limited resources and no safe modern transportation, she organized a journey over difficult terrain.

The children traveled with her toward safety, crossing mountains and moving through dangerous areas. Some were exhausted, frightened, and hungry. Gladys had to keep them together and find food and shelter while avoiding the dangers created by the war.

The journey became one of the most famous parts of her story. After days of travel, Gladys and the children eventually reached safety in the city of Xi'an. The experience demonstrated the extraordinary responsibility she had accepted. She was not simply caring for children in a mission home; she was protecting them during a war.

Gladys became known among local people by the Chinese name Ai-weh-deh, often translated as "Virtuous One." Her reputation grew because of her willingness to help people regardless of their background. She worked with Chinese communities and continued caring for children who had nowhere else to go.

After the war, Gladys continued her work in China for several years. Political changes eventually made it impossible for her to remain there. In 1953, she left China and moved to Hong Kong, where she continued serving children and people in need.

Gladys later established an orphanage in Taiwan. Her work there continued the mission she had begun in China: providing children with care, education, and a stable home. She remained closely involved in the lives of the children she served.

Her story eventually attracted international attention. Books, newspapers, and films introduced audiences outside China to her experiences. The 1958 film The Inn of the Sixth Happiness, starring Ingrid Bergman, was inspired by Gladys's life, although the movie changed or fictionalized some details of her story.

Gladys did not become famous because she had political authority or military power. Her influence came from years of ordinary service. She learned a new language, lived among people whose culture was unfamiliar to her, cared for children, and continued working when war made her surroundings dangerous.

Her faith was central to the way she understood her work. She believed that serving people was a practical expression of Christianity. Her life was therefore not built around fame or recognition. She focused on helping the people directly in front of her, especially children who had little ability to protect themselves.

Gladys Aylward died in Taiwan in 1970 at the age of sixty-seven. By then, she had spent decades serving people in China and Taiwan. The children she had cared for had grown up, many carrying memories of the woman who had devoted her life to them.

Her legacy is especially powerful because of how unlikely her journey seemed at the beginning. She had no university degree, no large organization behind her, and no guarantee of success. She simply believed that she had a purpose and refused to give up when other people told her that she was not qualified.

Gladys's story also shows that courage can appear in many different forms. Sometimes courage means crossing a dangerous country with children. Sometimes it means opening your home to someone who has nowhere else to go. Sometimes it means continuing to serve when the work becomes exhausting and frightening.

She did not stop because the circumstances were difficult. Instead, difficulty often became the reason she continued. During war, she protected children. During poverty, she provided food and shelter. When political changes forced her from one country, she found another place where she could continue serving.

Gladys Aylward's life is remembered as a story of faith, courage, perseverance, and compassion. She traveled across the world with very little, yet she eventually became a source of hope for hundreds of people. Her greatest legacy was not a building or an award. It was the lives of the children and families she helped.

Her story reminds us that a person's impact is not determined by how powerful they are when they begin. Gladys started as an ordinary young woman working as a domestic servant in England. Through determination and a willingness to serve, she built a life that reached far beyond what anyone could have expected.

Gladys Aylward chose to go where she believed she was needed. She crossed borders, learned a new language, faced war, protected children, and continued her mission for decades. Her life shows how one person's decision to care can become a legacy that survives long after that person is gone.
